Authenticity

A real bead should prove itself

Rudraksha is widely imitated and widely over-claimed. Here is exactly what you receive with a Rudrakhchya bead, and how to check any bead you are offered.

The certificate

Activated beads and Gaurishankar beads are sold with a certificate that describes that specific bead, not the product in general. For an energized bead you can ask us for close-up photographs or a video of the exact bead before you order. It records the things you can check with your own eyes and a ruler:

The bead's reference number, its mukhi count, its size and weight, and photographs of the bead matched to the paper. For activated beads, the consecration is recorded too.

Signs of a genuine bead

Natural, uneven furrows

  • Mukhi lines run from one end to the other
  • They are never perfectly even
  • The count matches what is claimed

A seed, not a mould

  • Bumpy, slightly irregular surface
  • Hole shows natural fibre, not a smooth plastic bore
  • No glue lines or seams along a furrow

Honest claims

  • The seller names the mukhi and the size
  • Photographs are of the bead you receive
  • No guaranteed results

A note on the water test. The common "sinks or floats" test is not reliable. Density varies between genuine beads, and fakes can be made to sink. Do not buy or reject a bead on that alone.

Going further

X-ray and lab reports

An X-ray shows the internal chambers of a bead, which confirms the mukhi count inside and shows whether it has been carved, filled or glued. For a common bead that is usually not needed; for a rare or high-value bead it can matter a great deal.

We provide X-ray observation reports for rare or high-value beads, and on request for others. Message us if you want one for the bead you are considering.
